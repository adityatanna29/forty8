# Advanced Adaptation, Scaling & Efficiency Techniques for Tabular ICL Foundation Models (2024 – Nov 2026): Gaps for TabTune

## Q1. Context selection and retrieval, and scaling ICL to 1M+ rows

### Takeaway
Retrieval-based local context (kNN) with retrieval-aware fine-tuning (LoCalPFN), and cluster-routed mixtures of fine-tuned experts (MixturePFN), are the most validated "context intelligence" techniques that TabTune lacks. Raw row scale has largely moved upstream: TabICLv2 (QASSMax, offloading, KV cache) and TabPFN-3/3.5 (1M rows, reduced KV cache, row chunking) now handle million-row tables natively. TabTune's job is therefore to *expose and orchestrate* these engine features (KV-cache reuse, offload, chunked and batched inference) uniformly across models, not to reinvent them.

### Cited Findings
**Retrieval / kNN context**
- **LoCalPFN** (Thomas et al., NeurIPS 2024). For each query it retrieves the k nearest neighbours as the TabPFN context ("TabPFN-kNN"), then fine-tunes end-to-end *with* the retrieved context. Reported SOTA on 95 TabZilla/OpenML datasets, even against tuned tree models. Ablations: naive FT followed by kNN at inference does **not** help, so joint retrieval-aware FT is essential. Raw standardized features are a workable retrieval space. The number of neighbours is the main hyperparameter. With max context 50 it beats RF, and with 500 it beats XGBoost (snippet) — [arXiv 2406.05207](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05207); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/c40daf14d7a6469e65116507c21faeb7-Abstract.html)
- Secondary review reports IQM AUC on medium and large datasets: LoCalPFN 0.968, TabPFN-kNN 0.955, vanilla TabPFN 0.927. **Unverified against the paper** — [themoonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/retrieval-fine-tuning-for-in-context-tabular-models)
- **MixturePFN** (ICLR 2025, "Mixture of In-Context Prompters for Tabular PFNs"). Clusters the training set, fine-tunes a specialised expert prompter per cluster, and routes each test point to its expert (sparse MoE over contexts). Reported best mean rank across 36 datasets against 19 baselines (snippet) — [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/3de60dc2ff7ac8866e805bbf457e2291-Abstract-Conference.html); [ICLR poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/31124)
- Caveat: per-query context selection (MixturePFN, kNN) breaks PFN-style batching and lowers inference throughput, according to the BETA authors (a competing method, so possibly biased) — [arXiv 2502.02527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.02527)
- Mechanistic support for retrieval: Rubachev et al. find that fine-tuning TabPFNv2 works *because* query·key dot products between test and context rows come to reflect target similarity better after FT. In other words, FT makes attention behave like a better retriever (snippet) — [arXiv 2506.08982](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08982v1)

**Context compression / prompt tuning**
- **TuneTables** (Feuer et al., NeurIPS 2024). Learns a small set of prompt embeddings that *replace* a large training context ("context optimization"). Tunes fewer than 5% of TabPFN parameters and is strong on 98 datasets. It does not beat TabPFN on small datasets because it overfits, and it is not uniformly better than GBDTs. It can also optimise a fairness objective. Code is public (full-FT and "short" modes) — [arXiv 2402.11137v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11137v3); [GitHub penfever/TuneTables](https://github.com/penfever/TuneTables/)
- **Bounded Context Management for TFMs on stream learning** (arXiv 2606.18677, 2026). Proposes "CURE" for bounded-memory context curation on frozen backbones (TabICL-v2, TabPFN-v2.5, TabDPT-v1, LimiX). Details not retrieved (snippet) — [arXiv 2606.18677](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.18677)

**Scaling to 1M+ rows**
- **TabFlex** (Zeng, Dinh, Kang, Mueller, ICML 2025). Replaces softmax attention with linear attention. SSM causality hurts ICL, while linear attention does not. Handles thousands of features, hundreds of classes, and millions of rows. Poker-hand (>1M rows) runs in about 5 s, and it is about 2x faster than TabPFN on small data. Three sub-models are selected by dataset size, and it is meant to be combined with dimensionality reduction and subsampling (snippet) — [PMLR v267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zeng25b.html); [arXiv 2506.05584](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05584v1)
- **TabICLv2** (arXiv 2602.11139, ICML 2026). QASSMax attention generalises beyond the pretraining context length. The authors claim it handles million-scale tables natively, without retrieval or distillation (snippet) — [arXiv 2602.11139](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11139v1). README (opened): benchmarked from 300 to 100K rows and up to 2K features. 500K+ rows are handled via CPU/disk offloading (`offload_mode="auto"`, `disk_offload_dir`), "a million samples in a few minutes" on modern GPUs, possibly with degraded accuracy. `kv_cache=True` caches training projections for repeated predict calls. `save(save_training_data=False)` with a KV cache helps privacy. 50K×100 fit+predict takes under 10 s on an H100, which the authors put at about 10x faster than TabPFN-2.5. Complexity is O(n²+nm²) — [GitHub soda-inria/tabicl](https://github.com/soda-inria/tabicl)
- **TabPFN-3** (Prior Labs, May 2026). Scales to 1M training rows on a single H100, up to 20x faster than TabPFN-2.5, using a reduced KV cache and row chunking. Base weights are under the TABPFN-3.0 licence (research and internal use). The "TabPFN-3-Plus (Thinking)" variant is API/enterprise only (snippet) — [arXiv 2605.13986](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13986); [AWS listing (envelope 1M×200, 100K×2K, 1K×20K)](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-b6rjndzxhbqww). TabPFN-3.5 (1M rows, 6K features, "Fast" variant) is reported only by Wikipedia and is **unverified** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TabPFN)
- **TabPFN library engine features** (opened, releases page, 2026): `fit_mode="fit_with_cache"` with `keep_cache_on_device` (CPU offload of per-estimator KV caches, v8.0.8). Chunked test-set inference via `TABPFN_MAX_BATCHED_TEST_ROWS` (default 32768) and `calculate_cache_size` (v8.1.0). `predict_proba_batched` scores **multiple datasets in one fused forward pass** (v8.1.0, regressor in v8.3.0). Importance-based feature subsampling with a shared ranking above 100K rows (v9.0.0). Peak preprocessing RSS on a 666,667×2,000 fit dropped from 42.7 GB to 12.0 GB (v8.5.0). TabPFN-3.5 became the default in v9.0.0, code moved to Apache-2.0, and 2.5/2.6/3 weights remain non-commercial — [GitHub PriorLabs/TabPFN releases](https://github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN/releases)

### Inferences
- TabTune should add a **`RetrievalContext` module**: a model-agnostic kNN (and optionally learned-embedding or FM-embedding) context selector, plus **retrieval-aware fine-tuning**, where FT episodes are built from each query's neighbours. This plugs directly into TabTune's existing episodic meta-FT and context-sampling pipeline. LoCalPFN's evidence says retrieval without retrieval-aware FT leaves a lot of the gain unrealised.
- MixturePFN-style **cluster-routed experts** fit naturally with TabTune's LoRA: one LoRA adapter per cluster, plus a router. This is cheap in parameters and reuses existing PEFT code.
- TuneTables-style **learned prompt/context tokens** are a "compressed context" PEFT mode. It is most useful for large datasets and for deploying without shipping the training data, a privacy angle that matches TabICL's `save_training_data=False`.
- For 1M+ rows, the gap is a **unified large-data inference layer**: KV-cache fit/predict separation, test-row chunking, CPU/disk offload, and multi-dataset fused batching, exposed consistently across TabPFN-3.x, TabICLv2 and others. TabTune's prediction cache caches *outputs*. A KV cache of *context projections* is a different and complementary thing.

### Gaps
- Could not verify whether LoCalPFN and MixturePFN code is public (GitHub not searched for them specifically).
- TabDPT (retrieval + real-data pretraining) and TabR (retrieval-augmented DL) were not retrieved in this session, so no sourced numbers are given.
- No sourced benchmark comparing kNN retrieval against TabPFN-3's native 1M-row context. It is unclear whether retrieval still helps once contexts are this long; this needs empirical testing.

## Q2. Fine-tuning advances (PEFT variants, TTT, continued pretraining, priors, FT recipes, multi-task)

### Takeaway
The 2025–26 evidence shows that *how* and *when* you fine-tune matters more than which PEFT variant you use. Fine-tune only medium-sized (1K–10K rows) or wide (>100 features) data, prefer PEFT or meta-FT over full SFT for stability and calibration, and use early stopping that restores weights. The larger wins come from **continued pretraining on curated real data** (Real-TabPFN; TabPFN-Wide for wide data), **encoder/adapter + bagging FT** (BETA), and **test-time training**. No primary DoRA results on tabular FMs were found.

### Cited Findings
- **On Finetuning Tabular Foundation Models** (Rubachev, Kotelnikov, Kartashev, Babenko; Yandex/HSE; arXiv 2506.08982, Jun 2025). For TabPFNv2, *full* FT is the most practical choice for time and effectiveness, and it did not consistently overfit on medium-scale data, unlike earlier TabPFNv1 findings. FT gives SOTA on academic i.i.d. splits but is less stable under gradual temporal shift or with rich feature sets, where prior methods remain better. Tested up to 50K rows on a single GPU (snippet) — [arXiv 2506.08982](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08982v1); [review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/on-finetuning-tabular-foundation-models)
- **Exploring Fine-Tuning for Tabular Foundation Models** (arXiv 2601.09654, 2026; appears to come from the TabTune/Orion authors' ecosystem, which is not confirmed). Below 1K rows, zero-shot is better and FT overfits. At 1K–10K rows, FT gives small consistent gains, especially meta-learning FT for TabPFN and OrionMSP. Datasets with more than 100 features benefit. Full SFT is the riskiest and often lowers accuracy *and calibration*. LoRA recovers the lost performance and limits overfitting (secondary review) — [themoonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/exploring-fine-tuning-for-tabular-foundation-models). This **conflicts in part** with Rubachev et al. on full FT; the models and setups differ.
- **KnowsTFM** (arXiv 2606.30258, 2026). Knowledge-informed FT of *small* TFMs. Reports that vanilla FT hurts large models: TabICL v2 drops from 0.763 zero-shot to 0.743 after vanilla FT, attributed to disrupting in-context behaviour (snippet) — [arXiv 2606.30258](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30258v1)
- **TabICL / TabPFN native FT APIs now include recipe guardrails** (opened). `FinetunedTabICLClassifier/Regressor` uses AdamW, cosine-with-warmup, gradient clipping and early stopping on a validation split, with `eval_metric` set to roc_auc, log_loss or accuracy, and auto-detects multi-GPU torchrun — [tabicl README](https://github.com/soda-inria/tabicl). The TabPFN FT classes add early stopping that **restores original weights if no epoch improves** (v8.1.0), `validation_frequency` (v8.3.0), multi-GPU DDP FT that splits estimator activations (v8.4.0), and `fit_with_differentiable_input` — [TabPFN releases](https://github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN/releases)
- **BETA, "TabPFN Unleashed"** (Liu & Ye, ICML 2025). Bagging and encoder-based fine-tuning: a lightweight trainable encoder (input adapter) maps arbitrary-dimensional data into several fixed-dimensional views, which reduces bias and handles high-dimensional data. Multiple lightweight encoders plus bootstrapped sampling reduce variance at low inference cost. It matches or beats SOTA on more than 200 classification datasets (snippet) — [PMLR v267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/liu25cn.html); [arXiv 2502.02527](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02527v1)
- **Real-TabPFN** (Garg, Ali, Hollmann, Purucker, Müller, Hutter; arXiv 2507.03971, ICML 2025 workshop). Continued pretraining of TabPFNv2 on a *small curated set of large real datasets* beats broader, noisier corpora such as CommonCrawl tables. Uses max context 20K (against 2,048 originally). Substantial gains on 29 OpenML AMLB datasets, beating TabPFNv2 on most of them. Promised open-source. A secondary source reports ROC-AUC rising from 0.954 to 0.976, which is **unverified** (snippet) — [arXiv 2507.03971](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.03971); [ICML workshop](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/47524)
- **TabPFN-Wide** (arXiv 2510.06162). Continued pretraining for extreme feature counts, citing Real-TabPFN as precedent. Details not retrieved (snippet) — [arXiv 2510.06162](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06162)
- **Test-time training**: Gozeten et al. (ICML 2025) analyse gradient-based TTT for ICL, training on the in-context demonstrations at inference time. On TabPFN, TTT substantially reduces the sample size needed for tabular classification at small cost (snippet) — [arXiv 2502.17361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17361v2)
- **Test-time compute without weight updates**: TabPFN-3 introduces test-time compute scaling. "TabPFN-3-Plus (Thinking)" improves Elo without updating weights, but it is API-only and its mechanism was not retrieved (snippet) — [arXiv 2605.13986](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13986)
- **Prior customisation**: TabAbax (arXiv 2611.00562, Nov 2026) builds on TabICLv2 and changes the *synthetic prior* and the column-row encoder rather than adding depth. Deeper ICL transformers give weak gains. Reported strong regression results on TabArena/BeyondArena (snippet) — [arXiv 2611.00562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00562). Also: "Towards Synthetic Data for Fine-tuning Tabular Foundation Models" (ICML 2025 workshop) — [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/47485) (title only).
- **PEFT/LoRA commentary**: a Qlik overview says most TFMs lose calibration after SFT, and that Orion-MSP prefers PEFT/LoRA for stability (secondary, vendor blog) — [Qlik blog](https://www.qlik.com/blog/the-shift-to-table-native-foundation-models)
- **KernelICL** (arXiv 2602.02162). Fine-tunes the TabICL embedding module with a kernel-regression head, making predictions inspectable as weighted neighbours (snippet) — [arXiv 2602.02162](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02162v1)

### Inferences
- **Highest-value FT addition: an "AutoFT policy" / recipe router.** Pick zero-shot, meta-FT, LoRA or full FT from n_rows, n_features, shift diagnostics and validation behaviour, with weight-restoring early stopping and **post-FT calibration** (temperature or threshold tuning toward the target metric). The evidence is consistent that no single FT mode wins everywhere and that SFT damages calibration. This is cheap to build on TabTune's existing FT modes.
- **Encoder/adapter FT (BETA-style)** is a new PEFT family, distinct from LoRA because it adapts the *input space*. It addresses high-dimensional data and plugs into TabTune's ensembling via bagged encoders.
- **Retrieval-aware FT** (Q1) and **TTT** (query-local gradient steps on the context at inference) are natural extensions of TabTune's episodic meta-FT engine.
- **Continued pretraining** (Real-TabPFN-style multi-dataset training on a user's curated corpus of in-domain tables) is a "domain FM" feature for enterprise users with many related tables. It is high value but compute-heavy, and it needs forgetting controls.
- DoRA and other LoRA variants: no tabular evidence found. Adding DoRA is a trivial PEFT config switch, but it should not be marketed as a gain without TabTune's own benchmarks.

### Gaps
- No primary-source DoRA/VeRA/IA³ results on any tabular FM were found (2024–2026).
- TabForestPFN (forest-based prior for ICL-transformer fine-tuning) could not be retrieved in this session. It is known from prior knowledge as den Breejen et al. 2024, but that is **unverified here**.
- Mitra (Amazon, an FT-optimised synthetic-prior FM) is mentioned in AutoGluon docs (see Q4), but its paper was not retrieved.
- Real-TabPFN weight release status was not confirmed.
- TabPFN-3 "Thinking" mechanism is unknown (proprietary).

## Q3. Efficiency (quantization, FlashAttention, compression/distillation, latency, memory)

### Takeaway
Two efficiency paths now have tabular-specific evidence. (a) **Low-precision attention and KV cache**: FP8 QKV attention gives up to 1.7x speedup with no relevant accuracy loss on TabPFN-v3 and TabICLv2, provided train and test rows are quantized consistently. fp8 and adaptive KV-cache dtypes, bf16 CPU autocast and torch.compile have shipped upstream. (b) **Structural compression and better distillation**: layer pruning plus adapters (TACTICL), two-stage generative-expansion distillation (GEAR), and hypernetwork weight generation (MotherNet). GEAR in particular could be a "clearly superior variant" of TabTune's averaged-soft-label distillation, but its numbers could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- **Attention Quantization for Tabular Foundation Models** (Kübler et al., arXiv 2609.13031, 2026). Argues that the attention *computation* (not weights or KV cache) is the right target. Quantizes Q/K/V to FP8 with a Triton kernel, reaching up to 1.7x over 16-bit kernels. No relevant accuracy loss on TabArena and BeyondArena for TabPFN-v3 and TabICLv2. **Accuracy collapses unless the quantization error of test rows matches that of training rows.** Biggest gains at hundreds of thousands of rows (snippet) — [arXiv 2609.13031](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13031)
- **Benchmarking Attention for Tabular Foundation Models** (ICML 2026). On H100 with tabular shapes, cuDNN SDPA is best for *column* attention at short and medium lengths, and FlashAttention-3 is best for *row* attention (snippet) — [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/71456)
- **Upstream TabPFN efficiency features** (opened): bf16 autocast on bf16-capable CPUs gives about 2x faster CPU inference at unchanged accuracy (v8.2.0). `kv_cache_dtype` with an fp8 option (v8.3.0). `kv_cache_precision="adaptive"`, with smaller host memory and saved files (v9.1.0). Layers are quantized during KV-cache construction to reduce peak GPU memory. Opt-in `enable_torch_compile` (v9.2.0, PyTorch ≥2.6) — [TabPFN releases](https://github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN/releases)
- **TACTICL** (arXiv 2608.10837, 2026). Task-aware compression: prunes layers from TabPFNv2, replaces them with lightweight task-trained adapters, and optionally fine-tunes the compressed model end-to-end (snippet; ratios and speedups not retrieved) — [arXiv 2608.10837](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10837v1). A related "End-to-End Compression for TFMs" paper (arXiv 2602.05649) is only known from a citation.
- **GEAR** (arXiv 2608.18849, 2026). "Generative Expansion and Real Anchoring", a two-stage distillation of TFMs: stage 1 distils on generatively expanded synthetic inputs, stage 2 anchors on real data (mechanism inferred from title and snippet; numbers **not retrieved**) — [arXiv 2608.18849](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18849)
- **TabPFN-2.5 distillation engine** (Prior Labs, Nov 2025). Per-dataset distillation into an MLP ("TabPFN-2.5-as-MLP") or a tree ensemble ("as-TreeEns"), keeping most of the accuracy at much lower latency. **Proprietary, licence-only, not in the API** (snippet) — [arXiv 2511.08667](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08667); [HN post](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/45838540)
- **MotherNet** (Müller, Curino, Ramakrishnan; ICLR 2025). A hypernetwork transformer trained on synthetic tasks that outputs the *weights* of a child MLP in one forward pass from a training set. The child beats GD-trained MLPs on small data, is competitive with TabPFN and GBDT, and is very cheap at inference. Limited to about 5K training rows by quadratic attention — [ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/bed8e95c6a99df485a6fc8b973e2d6cb-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2312.08598](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08598v2)
- **Batching across datasets**: `predict_proba_batched` runs a fused forward pass over multiple datasets (opened) — [TabPFN releases](https://github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN/releases). TabICL's `batch_size` controls how many ensemble members run together, trading memory for speed (opened) — [tabicl README](https://github.com/soda-inria/tabicl)

### Inferences
- TabTune should add an **`efficiency` config layer**: precision (bf16, fp8 attention, fp8/adaptive KV cache), attention backend selection (SDPA vs FA3 by axis), torch.compile, memory-budget-aware ensemble batch sizing, and offload. Kübler et al.'s train/test-consistent quantization is a correctness rule TabTune must enforce if it implements its own quantization.
- **Compression of the FM itself** (TACTICL-style pruning plus adapters) complements TabTune's student distillation. It keeps ICL behaviour and is useful when a GBDT or MLP student loses too much accuracy.
- **GEAR's generative-expansion stage** is the most likely "superior variant" of TabTune's public averaged-soft-label distillation. Using the FM's own generative or unsupervised mode (tabpfn-extensions `unsupervised` and `tabebm` can generate synthetic data, see Q5) to create transfer sets is a cheap approximation. Verify GEAR's numbers first.
- MotherNet-style amortised weight generation is lower priority: it is capped at about 5K rows and has no maintained ecosystem.

### Gaps
- Exact GEAR and TACTICL numbers, and whether their code is released, were not retrieved (arXiv blocked).
- No int8 weight-quantization study specific to tabular FMs was found. Kübler et al. argue weight quantization is the wrong target.
- No public numbers for TabPFN-2.5-as-MLP/TreeEns accuracy retention (the priorlabs.ai report was blocked).

## Q4. AutoML integration (AutoGluon / TabArena use of FMs, HPO for FMs)

### Takeaway
AutoGluon (1.4+) uses TFMs as members of a **heterogeneous, portfolio-based weighted ensemble** with GBDTs and deep tabular models. Its "extreme" preset is derived from the TabArena ensemble and includes TabPFNv2, TabICL, Mitra, TabDPT and TabM. Ensembles made only of TFMs hit a **diversity ceiling** (Lexsi Labs, ICML 2026). TabTune's six ensemblers would gain more from *cross-family* members (GBDT and TabM) and portfolio or HPO search than from more TFM-only strategies.

### Cited Findings
- AutoGluon 1.4 "extreme" preset: a new portfolio that improves on the TabArena ensemble (TabArena paper Fig. 6a). It needs TabPFNv2, TabICL and Mitra weights. "By using a weighted ensemble of different model types from TabArena, we can significantly outperform … AutoGluon 1.3". TFMs "are only applicable to a subset of datasets" and "still need tuning/ensembling to get the top spot" — [AutoGluon 1.4 release notes](https://auto.gluon.ai/dev/whats_new/v1.4.0.html)
- AutoGluon FM tutorial: the 'best' preset draws on TabPFNv2, TabICL, Mitra, TabDPT and TabM. 'extreme' is "massively better than 'best' on datasets <30000 samples" and requires a GPU. Mitra is pretrained on synthetic data and optimised for *fine-tuning* rather than ICL, and excels below 5K rows and 100 features — [AutoGluon FM tutorial (dev)](https://auto.gluon.ai/dev/tutorials/tabular/tabular-foundational-models.html); [1.5 version](https://auto.gluon.ai/1.5/tutorials/tabular/tabular-foundational-models.html)
- TabPFN-2.5 claims to match AutoGluon 1.4 tuned for 4 hours, including an ensemble that contains TabPFNv2 (vendor claim) — [arXiv 2511.08667](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08667)
- **Ensembling TFMs: A Diversity Ceiling and a Calibration Trap** (Tanna, Desai, Seth, Bouadi, Bouarour, Sankarapu; arXiv 2605.18696, ICML 2026, which appears to be the Lexsi/TabTune team). Six TFMs are nearly redundant, with mean pairwise Q-statistic 0.961, failing on the same instances because of the shared ICL-on-synthetic-priors recipe. Across six ensemble strategies on 153 OpenML tasks, the best (two-level cascade stacking) adds only +0.18% accuracy over the best single model at **253x compute**. Three ensembles tie with the best base model and three are significantly worse. LR-stacking has the worst log-loss rank because the meta-learner sharpens boundaries and destroys calibration, while convex combiners preserve calibration. The authors recommend greedy selection as the default (snippet) — [arXiv 2605.18696](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18696); [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/71554)
- TabPFN library HPO and calibration hooks: `TabPFNRegressor` accepts `eval_metric` and `tuning_config` (temperature calibration toward NLL or CRPS, v8.4.0). Checkpoints declare their trained softmax temperature, and invalid metric/threshold-tuning combinations now raise errors (v9.0.0) (opened) — [TabPFN releases](https://github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN/releases)
- tabpfn-extensions includes `bayesian_optimization` (TabPFN as a BO surrogate), Apache-2.0 (opened) — [tabpfn-extensions](https://github.com/PriorLabs/tabpfn-extensions)

### Inferences
- Add a **heterogeneous post-hoc ensembling / portfolio module**: TFM(s) plus GBDT plus TabM or another deep model, with Caruana greedy selection on OOF predictions and calibration-preserving combiners by default. This targets the diversity ceiling TabTune's own team has documented.
- Add **FM HPO**: search over inference-time hyperparameters (n_estimators, softmax temperature, preprocessing transforms, context size or subsampling, retrieval k) and FT hyperparameters (LR, epochs, LoRA rank), with a TFM-as-surrogate BO option (tabpfn-extensions BO).
- Add **metric-aware post-hoc calibration and threshold tuning** as a standard final stage, following TabPFN's `eval_metric`/`tuning_config` pattern.

### Gaps
- The TabArena paper itself (methodology for TFM portfolios and post-hoc ensembling) was not opened in this session.
- No source quantified how much the GBDT members specifically add over TFM-only ensembles in AutoGluon.

## Q5. Feature engineering and preprocessing for FMs (LLM FE, feature selection, text/datetime)

### Takeaway
Upstream models now include basic datetime and text handling (TabPFN v9: `TRANSFORM_DATES`, `TRANSFORM_TEXT` via skrub). Embed-then-PCA text pipelines bottleneck information, and frozen-encoder text adapters (ICML 2026) or text-native models (TabSTAR) do better. LLM-driven feature generation (CAAFE, LLM-FE) gives measured gains for TabPFN but carries bias risks. Wide-data handling is via importance-based feature subsampling, encoder adapters (BETA) or wide-pretrained models (TabPFN-Wide).

### Cited Findings
- TabPFN v9.0.0 (opened): `TRANSFORM_DATES` expands datetime into calendar features. `TRANSFORM_TEXT` encodes text columns with `skrub.StringEncoder`. `SAMPLE_SUBSAMPLING_METHOD="majority_downsample"` handles imbalanced or zero-inflated targets. Importance-based feature subsampling with a shared ranking above 100K rows. `n_estimators="auto"` scales the ensemble for feature coverage on wide data (v8.3.0) — [TabPFN releases](https://github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN/releases)
- TabPFN docs: the local package can't interpret raw datetimes, so extract year, month and day-of-week and add a running index for temporal data. The API auto-detects and embeds dates — [Prior Labs docs: feature engineering](https://docs.priorlabs.ai/improving-performance/feature-engineering.md) (snippet)
- **Towards Pretraining Text Encoders for TabPFN** (arXiv 2606.04876, ICML 2026). The embed-plus-PCA pipeline is an information bottleneck. Proposes a "TabPFN Text Adapter" that keeps the encoder and TabPFN frozen and trains only a small adapter (snippet) — [arXiv 2606.04876](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04876); [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/71492)
- **TabSTAR** (NeurIPS 2025). A text-field TFM that unfreezes the text encoder and uses target-aware tokens for task-specific embeddings (snippet) — [arXiv 2505.18125](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18125v2). **ConTextTab** (SAP) pairs a frozen LM with a light projector and reportedly trails many models on non-text TabArena tasks (snippet, via the text-encoder paper) — [arXiv 2606.04876](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04876)
- **LLM-FE** (arXiv 2503.14434). LLMs act as evolutionary optimisers for feature generation, with gains for XGBoost, MLP *and TabPFN* across LLM backbones (snippet) — [arXiv 2503.14434](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.14434). **CAAFE**: iterative LLM feature generation with textual explanations (snippet, same search). A Freiburg group warns that LLM bias in generated features can hurt performance — [Freiburg ML](https://ml.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/?p=4353)
- tabpfn-extensions (opened, Apache-2.0): `interpretability` (SHAP plus feature selection), `pval_crt` (conditional-randomisation-test feature relevance p-values), `embedding` (TabPFN sample embeddings, usable as a retrieval space), `image` (DINOv3 features for image columns), `unsupervised` (synthetic data generation and outlier detection), `tabebm` (TabPFN-energy-based data augmentation), `many_class` (more classes than the checkpoint supports) — [tabpfn-extensions](https://github.com/PriorLabs/tabpfn-extensions)

### Inferences
- TabTune should add a **model-agnostic preprocessing layer**: datetime expansion, text-column encoding (start with skrub/sentence embeddings and offer a trainable text adapter), and a **many-class wrapper** (ECOC/hierarchical) so that every supported FM gets these, not only TabPFN.
- **FM-native feature selection** (permutation or SHAP or CRT importance computed with the FM itself) for data wider than the pretraining range (TabICL README: performance may drop beyond about 100 features).
- **LLM feature engineering** (CAAFE or LLM-FE) is an optional plugin. It is medium value, needs LLM API access, and carries the documented bias risks.
- **Generative augmentation** (TabEBM or FM unsupervised generation) is a stronger alternative to SMOTE in TabTune's resampling module, and it also feeds distillation transfer sets.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head of CAAFE vs LLM-FE vs no-FE specifically on TabPFN-3 or TabICLv2 was found.
- TabSTAR and ConTextTab licences and code status were not verified.

### Cross-cutting inferences
- Several 2026 papers (diversity-ceiling ensembling, "Exploring Fine-Tuning") appear to come from the TabTune/Lexsi team itself. Their findings support prioritising **FT policy and calibration (#2)** and **heterogeneous ensembling (#4)** over adding more TFM-only ensemble strategies.
- Upstream libraries (TabPFN v8–v9, TabICLv2) now ship KV caching, chunking, fp8, compile, batched multi-dataset inference and FT guardrails. TabTune's differentiation should be **cross-model uniformity and orchestration** (one API over TabPFN-3.x, TabICLv2, Orion, Mitra and TabDPT), plus methods upstream lacks: retrieval-aware FT, MoE contexts, encoder-bagging, GEAR-style distillation, and portfolio ensembling.
- **Licensing**: TabPFN-2.5, 2.6 and 3 weights are non-commercial and TabPFN-3-Plus is API-only. TabICLv2 is "permissive". tabpfn-extensions and TabPFN code are Apache-2.0. TabTune modules should be model-agnostic so that commercial users can run them on permissively licensed backbones.
