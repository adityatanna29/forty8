# Ship breadth publicly, keep trust proprietary

Public TabTune should grow in two directions. First, it should cover the models released in late 2026: NVIDIA Kumo Tabular, the LimiX-2 and TabDPT-Turbo checkpoints and TabSTAR on the tabular side, and TimesFM 2.5, Timestral, t0, the Toto 2.0 FT checkpoint and IBM's Granite suite on the time-series side. Second, it should add the commodity modules that competitors already ship: official benchmark runners, licence-filtered presets, a first explainability layer, embeddings, a large-data inference and efficiency layer, unsupervised and generative tools, and a deployment path. Internal TabTune should first rebase onto public main, which brings in time series, TabPFN v3.5, Mitra v2, Causilo, TabLDM and the CLI. After that it should put its effort where the 2024–2026 literature is thin or immature and where Internal is already ahead: Shapley-interaction and recontextualisation attribution in TabAttribute, generative-expansion distillation for PAD, heterogeneous and time-series Bayesian Competence Fusion, a privacy and fairness trust suite, an automatic fine-tuning policy, and domain continued pretraining. The split follows the evidence. Upstream vendors (Prior Labs' tabpfn-extensions, AutoGluon 1.6, Darts) already offer the commodity features, so withholding them gains nothing. The literature, by contrast, has no dedicated work on in-context data valuation, unlearning or attribution-driven privacy risk for tabular foundation models, which is where Internal can lead. Licences matter as much as leaderboards. A large share of the strongest new checkpoints (LimiX-2, TabPFN-2.5 and later, TimesFM 3, TimeH2O) are non-commercial, so every addition needs a licence flag, and the proprietary version in particular has to default to commercially usable weights.

> **Evidence caveat. Read this before acting on any number in this report.** arxiv.org, huggingface.co, tabarena.ai, priorlabs.ai, auto.gluon.ai, mistral.ai and several vendor and news sites were blocked (DNS) in the research environment. Apart from a few GitHub pages that were opened directly (TabArena, TabReD, fev, TSB-AD, AutoGluon releases, tabpfn-extensions, the TabPFN releases page, the TabICL README, PyTorch Frame, Darts), **almost every figure below comes from search-engine snippets and is a vendor or author self-report.** None of the live leaderboards (TabArena, GIFT-Eval, fev-bench) could be opened. Where sources disagree, this report shows the disagreement and does not pick a winner. Before any public claim or licence decision, verify the leaderboard positions, parameter counts and licence texts against the primary pages.

## Leaderboard claims conflict, so licence and fit should drive model choice

Late 2026 brought a burst of releases, each claiming first place on some board, and the claims cannot all be true on any one snapshot. On tabular data, a September 2026 blog put Google TabFM first on TabArena at about 1800 Elo. An October post had **LimiX-2 leading at 1971**, with the top five "not settled" ([thetabularguy](https://thetabularguy.substack.com/p/how-to-pick-a-tabular-foundation); [Qlik](https://www.qlik.com/blog/results-for-tabular-machine-learning)). LimiX-2's own materials report **1935** on TabArena ([ai-tldr](https://ai-tldr.dev/releases/stable-ai-limix-2/)). Prior Labs says TabPFN-3.5 is first on both TabArena and BeyondArena ([metavert](https://metavert.io/tabular-foundation-models)). An older snapshot named RealTabPFN-2.5 the top foundation model ([mindfulmodeler](https://mindfulmodeler.substack.com/p/the-state-of-tabular-foundation-models)). A June article gave TabPFN-3 1673 Elo on board v0.1.4 against 1375 for XGBoost ([ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/tabular-foundation-models-tabpfn-vs-xgboost-enterprise-2026/)). These figures come from different board versions, so their Elo scales are not comparable.

Time series is no cleaner. H2O.ai claims TimeH2O-v1 is **#1 of 32 on fev-bench with a 91.9% win rate** as of 23 Oct 2026 ([cfotech](https://cfotech.com.au/story/h2o-ai-launches-timeh2o-v1-forecasting-model-tops-fev-bench)). The Forecasting Company claims t0-gamma is **#1 of 30 with a 91.6% win rate and a 49.42% skill score** as of 13 Oct ([TFC blog](https://www.theforecastingcompany.com/blog/t0-gamma/)). Mistral claims Timestral-209M is #1 on GIFT-Eval, fev-bench and TIME on all six metrics ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/timestral/)). Datadog says Toto 2.0 leads the foundation models on GIFT-Eval ([Toto 2.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20119v2)). The t0 paper puts TimesFM-3.0 and Granite PatchTST-FM above t0-beta on GIFT-Eval ([t0 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24559)). An undated aggregator lists a different system first by MASE rank ([TSFM.ai](https://tsfm.ai/benchmarks/gift-eval)). Relational data has the same problem: Stanford's RelArena-α material calls TabPFN-Rel the "top-ranked model submission" and also calls RT-PluRel "the #1 system" ([STAR RelArena](https://star-project.stanford.edu/relarena/)).

The practical consequence is that no single model can be called state of the art, and **an integration library gains more from coverage, reproducibility and licence clarity than from backing one winner**. This favours TabTune, because a fair, reproducible runner across all contenders is exactly what the field lacks. TALENT had to fix TFM wrappers that "silently ran a different model or configuration than intended" ([TALENT PR #115](https://github.com/LAMDA-Tabular/TALENT/pull/115)). That is a warning that applies to every new TabTune adapter.

## Public gets the September–October 2026 model wave

### One clear tabular gap, three cheap upgrades and a text model

TALENT's September 2026 update added seven tabular foundation models, and public TabTune already has six of them. The missing one is **NVIDIA Kumo Tabular**. It is a single-pass in-context learner "inspired by TabICL and TabPFN", pretrained on structural-causal-model tables, released with **OpenMDW-1.1 weights (commercial use allowed) and Apache-2.0 code**, and reported by NVIDIA as state of the art on TabArena's accuracy-efficiency Pareto front ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/kumo-tabular); [SDK table](https://nvidia.github.io/structured-data-models/api/models.html); [TALENT](https://github.com/LAMDA-Tabular/TALENT)). It is the only new near-top single-table model with commercial weights. That makes it the top public addition and, once inherited through the rebase, a valuable commercial backbone for Internal. Whether NVIDIA's `structured-data-models` library exposes training hooks for TabTune's meta-learning, SFT and LoRA paths is unverified.

The other near-term additions are upgrades to adapters TabTune already has. **LimiX-2** (400M parameters, one checkpoint for classification, regression and imputation) is open-sourced but under a **non-commercial licence** ([Pandaily](https://pandaily.com/stable-ai-limix-2-structured-data-foundation-model); [LimiX GitHub](https://github.com/limix-ldm-ai/LimiX)). **TabDPT v1.2 "Turbo"** (June 2026, described by its authors as the fastest model overall) and **v1.3** (September 2026) are on PyPI, though the licence of these versions is not stated ([PyPI tabdpt](https://pypi.org/project/tabdpt/)). AutoGluon 1.6 already ships TabDPT-Turbo in its commercially usable `extreme` preset ([AutoGluon releases](https://github.com/autogluon/autogluon/releases)). **TabSTAR** (MIT per GitHub, though an older HF diff says cc-by-4.0; a 47.3M-parameter checkpoint) fine-tunes its text encoder end to end and is reported to beat CARTE on 93% of classification splits and 36% of regression splits ([TabSTAR GitHub](https://github.com/alanarazi7/TabSTAR); [HF](https://huggingface.co/alana89/TabSTAR); [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18125)). Today ContextTab is TabTune's only text-aware model, and one 2026 paper reports that it trails many models on non-text TabArena tasks ([Text-encoder paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04876)).

Relational data is the main capability gap. The cheapest route is a **deep-feature-synthesis flattening front-end**, modelled on Prior Labs' TabPFN-Rel, which "flattens the database with deep feature synthesis and predicts in context with TabPFN-3" ([STAR RelArena](https://star-project.stanford.edu/relarena/); [tabpfn-rel cookbook](https://docs.priorlabs.ai/cookbook/relational_predictions_tabpfn_rel)). Built model-agnostically, it would feed any TabTune backbone, including the commercially usable ones. The native follow-up is Stanford's **Relational Transformer** family (22M-parameter RT, 86M RT-PluRel, RT-J). It has open HF checkpoints and reportedly reaches **93% of fully supervised AUROC in one forward pass** ([STAR RT](https://star-project.stanford.edu/rt/); [HF rt-j](https://huggingface.co/stanford-star/rt-j)). Its licence was not found, and it needs a multi-table input abstraction that TabTune's fit/predict API does not have yet.

### Time series should add commercial checkpoints first, then breadth

The most urgent time-series item is a licence problem, not a leaderboard one. **TimesFM 3, already in public TabTune, carries the timesfm-non-commercial-license-v1.0 (no production use, no mirroring). TimesFM 2.5 is Apache-2.0** ([mindstudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/timesfm-licensing-commercial-use); [aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/google-timesfm-3-tops-forecast-benchmarks-non-commercial)). Adding the 2.5 checkpoint to the existing loader is a small job that gives users a production-legal TimesFM, and Internal will need exactly that once it rebases. The **Toto 2.0 2.5B-FT** checkpoint is similarly cheap. It is Apache-2.0 and Datadog-reported to lead GIFT-Eval among foundation models, with a BOOM CRPS of about 0.349 from a third-party import ([Datadog](https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/ai/toto-2/); [benchmarklist](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/boom/)).

Two genuinely new models follow. **Timestral** (Mistral, 15 Oct 2026; 5M to 209M parameters) offers a 32,768-step context, uni- and multivariate targets, and past and known-future covariates including categorical ones. Its licence is a Modified MIT; a third party mentions a monthly-revenue clause that has not been confirmed ([Mistral docs](https://docs.mistral.ai/models/timestral-1-0-209m.md); [braindetox](https://braindetox.kr/en/posts/timestral_mistral_time_series_model.html)). The **t0 family** installs with pip as `tfc-t0`, has Apache-2.0 code, and has open weights whose licence is unconfirmed. Covariates add **+6.3 fev-bench skill points on known-future tasks** ([t0 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24559); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/tfc-t0/)). Darts already ships T0Model, PatchTSTFMModel, TimesFM3Model and TiRex2Model ([Darts](https://github.com/unit8co/darts)), so these are parity items rather than differentiators.

For breadth, the best value is **IBM's Granite suite**: one `tsfm_public` integration brings in PatchTST-FM (second on GIFT-Eval in the t0 paper's table), TTM-r3, FlowState and TSPulse. IBM says the family covers forecasting, classification, anomaly detection, imputation and similarity search without a GPU ([IBM Granite TS docs](https://www.ibm.com/granite/docs/models/time-series); [t0 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24559)). Be careful with FlowState's licence: the HF YAML says Apache-2.0, but the newer checkpoint is labelled "Research checkpoint (non-commercial use)" ([HF commit](https://huggingface.co/ibm-research/flowstate/commit/f63b6711b6f3a1f44cec27b2afbe7abbd11cf4b1)). **Sundial** (Apache-2.0, ICML 2025 oral) generates real sample paths via flow matching instead of fixed quantiles, which adds ensemble diversity ([Sundial](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00816v3); [gitee mirror](https://gitee.com/chenlinos/Sundial)). **Moirai 2.0** is a widely cited baseline with a fine-tuning library, but one source reports a CC BY-NC licence ([Salesforce blog](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/moirai-2-0/); [ai-souken](https://www.ai-souken.com/article/what-is-time-series-foundation-model)).

## Public modules close parity gaps that competitors already ship

The module gaps in public TabTune can be read straight off competitors' feature lists. **tabpfn-extensions** (Apache-2.0) ships interpretability, many-class handling, unsupervised generation and outlier detection, embeddings, image columns, TabEBM augmentation, CRT p-values and Bayesian-optimisation surrogates, but for one vendor's model ([tabpfn-extensions](https://github.com/PriorLabs/tabpfn-extensions)). **AutoGluon 1.6** ships a commercially usable `extreme` portfolio (Nori, TabICLv2, TabDPT-Turbo, TabPrep-LGBM), a separate `noncommercial` preset with TabPFN-3, and a `validation_structure` argument for grouped or temporal data ([AutoGluon releases](https://github.com/autogluon/autogluon/releases)). **Darts** ships conformal intervals for pretrained models, backtesting, an anomaly-detection module and SHAP ([Darts](https://github.com/unit8co/darts)). TabTune's value proposition is to provide these across roughly 30 models behind one API. Each of them is commodity, so none needs protecting in Internal.

**Benchmark runners come first**, because they also answer the leaderboard confusion above. TabArena is Apache-2.0 and accepts new methods via TabArena-Lite plus a PR. BeyondArena extends it to **142 IID, temporal and grouped datasets** and "will supersede TabArena-v0.1" ([TabArena GitHub](https://github.com/autogluon/tabarena)). TabReD downloads with a single `uvx` command and adds temporal shift ([TabReD](https://github.com/yandex-research/tabred)). fev's Task API makes a forecasting wrapper simple ([fev](https://github.com/autogluon/fev)), and TSB-AD installs with pip ([TSB-AD](https://github.com/TheDatumOrg/TSB-AD)). Internal already has a BeyondArena loader. Because BeyondArena is a public benchmark, that loader should be upstreamed rather than duplicated. TabularLeaderboard should follow TabArena's protocol (8-fold CV, Elo, improvability) so its numbers are comparable to the public board. Runners also need per-model lists of datasets each model has already seen: TALENT-274 drops development datasets, and Chronos-2 and TTM were pretrained on parts of GIFT-Eval's training data ([2607.23146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23146)).

**Explainability v1** should close public TabTune's single largest gap. The cleanest approach is to upstream the commodity layer of Internal's TabAttribute (SHAP and LIME behind `pipeline.explain`) and add **shapiq**. shapiq offers an imputation game and a TabPFN-native "remove-and-recontextualize" game, and `index="k-SII", max_order=2` gives pairwise interactions ([Prior Labs interpretability](https://docs.priorlabs.ai/capabilities/interpretability); [shapiq NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/eb3a9313405e2d4175a5a3cfcd49999b-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf)). CRT feature-relevance p-values from `pval_crt` cover statistical significance, which neither version has today ([tabpfn-extensions](https://github.com/PriorLabs/tabpfn-extensions)). Upstreaming keeps the public and internal APIs identical, so the rebase stays conflict-free while support-set attribution, gradient methods and faithfulness evaluation remain proprietary. One integration hazard: before shapiq v1.3.0, a TabPFN model could be left fitted on a feature subset after an explanation ran ([Socket.dev changelog](https://socket.dev/pypi/package/shapiq)).

**Scaling now belongs to orchestration, not new architecture.** TabPFN-3 handles 1M rows on one H100 and runs up to 20x faster than 2.5 ([TabPFN-3 report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13986)). TabICLv2 offers CPU or disk offload and a `kv_cache=True` option, and runs 50K×100 fit-plus-predict in under 10 s on an H100 ([TabICL README](https://github.com/soda-inria/tabicl)). TabPFN's library adds fused multi-dataset batching, bf16 CPU autocast (about 2x), fp8 and adaptive KV-cache dtypes and torch.compile ([TabPFN releases](https://github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN/releases)). FP8 QKV attention gives **up to 1.7x speedup with no relevant accuracy loss on TabPFN-v3 and TabICLv2, but accuracy collapses unless train and test rows are quantized consistently** ([Kübler et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13031)). TabTune's existing prediction cache stores outputs. A uniform layer that separates fitting the context KV cache from prediction, chunks rows, offloads and selects precision would complement that cache and work the same way across all models.

**Ensembling should become heterogeneous.** The team's own ICML 2026 paper finds six TFMs nearly redundant (mean pairwise Q-statistic **0.961**). The best of six ensemble strategies adds only **+0.18% accuracy at 253x compute**, and LR-stacking destroys calibration ([Diversity ceiling](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18696)). AutoGluon's preset mixes TFMs with GBDTs and TabM and is described as "massively better" than 'best' below 30,000 rows ([AutoGluon FM tutorial](https://auto.gluon.ai/dev/tutorials/tabular/tabular-foundational-models.html)). The public addition is therefore cross-family members, greedy Caruana selection on out-of-fold predictions, calibration-preserving combiners as the default, and licence-filtered `commercial`/`noncommercial` presets built on the registry.

**Adaptation is TabTune's public identity, so the validated adaptation methods belong there too.** LoCalPFN's kNN context with retrieval-aware fine-tuning reports state of the art on 95 datasets. Its ablation shows that naive fine-tuning followed by kNN at inference does not help, so retrieval-aware fine-tuning is required ([LoCalPFN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05207)). BETA's bagged input encoders match or beat state of the art on more than 200 datasets and handle high-dimensional data ([BETA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/liu25cn.html)). TuneTables replaces the context with learned prompts while tuning under 5% of parameters ([TuneTables](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11137v3)). Two cheap guardrails also belong in public: weight-restoring early stopping and post-fine-tuning temperature or threshold calibration. Upstream libraries already ship them ([TabPFN releases](https://github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN/releases)), and the evidence that SFT damages calibration is consistent ([Qlik](https://www.qlik.com/blog/the-shift-to-table-native-foundation-models); [2601.09654 review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/exploring-fine-tuning-for-tabular-foundation-models)).

## Internal deepens attribution, distillation and trust where literature is thin

Internal's first job is catching up. It sits on public 0.2.0 and so lacks the entire time-series stack, TabPFN v3.5, Mitra v2, Causilo, TabLDM and the CLI. **Rebasing onto public main (Now, L effort) is the precondition for everything else.** It also brings in every public "Now" model above at no extra cost. After that, Internal should push the three strengths it already has, attribution, distillation and fusion, and trust, into areas where the literature search found little or no prior work.

**TabAttribute v2.** Internal's attribution already goes beyond what tabpfn-extensions offers. The new value lies in three additions. First, **Shapley interaction indices** (k-SII, FSII, STII). Second, the **recontextualisation value function** as an option inside the faithfulness and disagreement benchmark. Prior Labs notes that recontextualisation is much slower because the KV cache cannot be reused across coalitions ([Prior Labs docs](https://docs.priorlabs.ai/capabilities/interpretability)), so the comparison between imputation and recontextualisation is itself a research-grade evaluation axis. Third, **adapter coverage** for the models arriving through the rebase and the public "Now" list (Kumo Tabular, LimiX-2, TabDPT v1.3, TabPFN v3.5, Mitra v2) plus OrionMoE. Attribution for time-series forecasters is a natural extension, since Darts already ships SHAP for forecasting models ([Darts](https://github.com/unit8co/darts)). Internal's support-set and data-valuation methods (LOO, kNN-Shapley, Datamodels, TracIn) appear to be **ahead of the published literature**: the search found no dedicated 2024–2026 paper on in-context data valuation for tabular foundation models. That is an argument for keeping these methods proprietary or publishing them on Lexsi's own terms. Glass-box ICL models (GAMformer; in-context kernel regression, [arXiv 2602.02162](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02162v1)) make useful ground-truth comparators for faithfulness testing.

**PAD v2 and FM compression.** PAD already improves on public's averaged-soft-label distillation. The two newest candidate techniques extend it. **GEAR** uses two stages, distilling first on generatively expanded inputs and then anchoring on real data ([GEAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18849)). **TACTICL** prunes TabPFNv2 layers and replaces them with task-trained adapters, keeping ICL behaviour when a student loses too much accuracy ([TACTICL](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10837v1)). Neither paper's numbers could be retrieved. A cheap first approximation of GEAR is to generate PAD transfer sets with the FM's own generator (TabEBM, or TabPFN unsupervised). The competitive reference is Prior Labs' **TabPFN-2.5-as-MLP/TreeEns distillation engine, which is proprietary, licence-only and not in the API** ([arXiv 2511.08667](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08667)). That a commercial equivalent exists confirms that distillation has value worth protecting. ONNX export of `pad_mlp` and `pad_gbdt` students then gives Internal a CPU deployment story that the transformers themselves lack. The search found no ONNX or TensorRT export for TabPFN-class models; Prior Labs itself steers large-data and CPU serving towards its distilled MLP/tree engine ([Prior Labs best practices](https://docs.priorlabs.ai/best-practices)).

**BCF v3: heterogeneous and time-series fusion.** The diversity-ceiling result (Q=0.961, +0.18% at 253x compute) implies that fusing TFM teachers with each other has little headroom ([Diversity ceiling](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18696)). Bayesian Competence Fusion should therefore admit GBDT and TabM teachers, where AutoGluon's portfolio evidence suggests real complementarity ([AG 1.4](https://auto.gluon.ai/dev/whats_new/v1.4.0.html)). On time series, competence-weighted routing is where the agentic and routing literature reports its gains. TimeRouter routes among existing TSFMs and reports state-of-the-art MASE on GIFT-Eval ([TimeRouter](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11625)), and an LLM-orchestrated ensemble reports beating leading TSFMs on CRPS and MASE ([2512.16022](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16022)). BCF over SeriesFeaturizer features is a proprietary version of the same idea, and it builds directly on an existing Internal asset.

**AutoFT policy.** No fine-tuning mode wins everywhere. Below 1K rows zero-shot is better. Between 1K and 10K rows, or above 100 features, fine-tuning helps. Vanilla fine-tuning can hurt large models, for example **TabICLv2 dropping from 0.763 to 0.743** ([2601.09654 review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/exploring-fine-tuning-for-tabular-foundation-models); [KnowsTFM](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30258v1)). A router that picks zero-shot, meta-FT, LoRA or full fine-tuning from size, width, shift diagnostics and validation behaviour turns the team's own fine-tuning studies into product IP. The commodity guardrails underneath it go to public.

**Trust suite: privacy, fairness attribution, audit.** 2026 preprints show that tabular foundation models leak context membership. TabPATE finds TabPFN more vulnerable and proposes a PATE-style DP defence with near-random attack success at moderate ε ([TabPATE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31474)). AMIA's attention-based attack finds TabPFN, TabICL and TabDPT as susceptible as traditional models ([AMIA](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26021)). PrivTab notes that standard TFMs give no formal guarantee ([PrivTab](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10068)). **No paper formalises unlearning for tabular foundation models.** For pure ICL, dropping a context row together with its cached KV is exact unlearning. For fine-tuned models it is not, and TabTune supports both. Three things belong in Internal: an MIA audit harness, DP-ICL wrappers and certified context deletion. In addition, Internal's TracIn and LOO scores can serve as **per-row MIA risk indicators**, which matches AMIA's "high-risk query" framing. On fairness, uncertainty-based demonstration selection consistently improves group fairness ([Fair-TabICL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09503)). Applying support-set attribution to group disparity, to find which context rows drive it, is a combination the search did not find in the literature. A generated trust report (calibration, fairness, MIA risk, shift, contamination) packages all of this for enterprise buyers.

A reasonable objection is that retrieval-aware fine-tuning or MixturePFN-style experts could also give Internal an edge, and OrionMoE's MoE design is close to MixturePFN's routing. Weighed against that, these methods are adaptation techniques with published code paths (TuneTables is public; the LoCalPFN and MixturePFN code is unverified). Adaptation is what the "TabTune" name promises, so keeping them out of the public library would weaken its pitch more than it would protect Internal. If the team wants more separation, cluster-routed LoRA experts on top of OrionMoE are the one adaptation item worth keeping proprietary.

## Licences split the model zoo into commercial and research tiers

Licence status is the deciding constraint for a proprietary product, and it is moving fast. Prior Labs moved the `tabpfn` package to Apache-2.0 at v9.0.0, but **TabPFN-2.5 and later weights remain non-commercial**. TabPFN-3 is under a research and internal-evaluation licence, and the Plus and Thinking variants are API/VPC only ([TabPFN releases](https://github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN/releases); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TabPFN); [Prior Labs models](https://docs.priorlabs.ai/models)). Internal therefore already contains TabPFN v2.6 and v3 weights it cannot deploy commercially without a Prior Labs licence, and the rebase adds TabPFN v3.5 and TimesFM 3 to that list. The registry should block these weights in commercial deployment profiles, not merely warn. AutoGluon's split between `extreme` and `noncommercial` presets is the industry template ([AutoGluon releases](https://github.com/autogluon/autogluon/releases)).

A TabPFN-specific module does not. This argues for building every new module against TabTune's common adapter interface rather than against one vendor's extension API.

## Conclusion

This literature changes where TabTune's advantage lies. In 2024 it lay in having the models. By late 2026 every competitor integrates the same models within weeks, vendors post conflicting first-place claims, and the strongest checkpoints increasingly come with non-commercial weights. What stays scarce is trustworthy comparison (parity-tested adapters, protocol-faithful runners, lists of datasets each model has seen), licence-correct deployment, and methods that work across vendors. Public TabTune should own the first two, because they build community trust and cost little to give away. Internal should own the third where the literature has nothing published: in-context data valuation, attribution-driven privacy risk, certified context unlearning, and competence-based fusion across model families.

The team's own diversity-ceiling paper is the strongest strategic signal in the survey. If ICL foundation models trained on similar synthetic priors fail on the same rows, then adding more of them gives sharply diminishing returns. The durable gains are in combining different model families (BCF v3 and portfolios), in explaining and auditing what a model's context is doing (TabAttribute v2 and the privacy suite), and in compressing that behaviour into deployable students (PAD v2). All three are Internal strengths. Before any of these numbers reaches a customer, re-run the claims in this report on unblocked primary sources or on TabTune's own benchmark runners.
